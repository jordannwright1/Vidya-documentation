# Vidya OS

Vidya is an AI data-analysis and research agent. Upload a dataset or ask a question, and Vidya plans the approach, writes and runs the code itself, fixes its own mistakes, and hands you back a written answer with charts — no notebook, no copy-pasting, no debugging on your end.

🔗 **Try it now:** [navi-os.cc](https://navi-os.cc) — no account needed to start.

## Getting started

Open the chat and start typing — a temporary account is provisioned for you automatically in the background, with no sign-up form. It comes with a smaller daily quota than a full account, and is cleaned up automatically after it's been inactive for a while.

Create a real (still free) account when you want:
- Your chat history and memory to persist between visits
- A higher daily query limit
- Subagents and external service connections

## What you can do

### Analyze a dataset

1. Upload a CSV or Excel file.
2. Ask your question in plain English — e.g. *"which product category grew the fastest this quarter?"*
3. Vidya writes and runs the analysis and gives you a written answer plus any relevant charts.

If the first attempt fails, Vidya doesn't just hand the error back to you. It diagnoses what went wrong, researches a fix if needed, rewrites the code, and tries again automatically — only asking you for help once it's genuinely stuck, and telling you exactly what it needs.

### Ask a research question

You don't need a dataset to ask Vidya something. If your question needs current, real-world information — a price, a ranking, anything that could have a different answer tomorrow than today — Vidya searches the web and cites its sources. Ask a timeless question instead and it just answers directly, no search needed.

The clearest value is practical rather than transformative: AI helps people complete some tasks faster, supports analysis and product development, and is beginning to improve business productivity. But the gains are uneven, and adoption or positive perceptions do not by themselves prove lasting value.

**Where value is showing up**

- **Time and capacity on specific tasks.** AI can draft or summarize material, help analyze information, and assist with code or design work. The OECD's review of experimental research reports gains across productivity, innovation, and entrepreneurship, while stressing that results depend on the task and the user's expertise; human oversight matters, especially for complex work. ([The effects of generative AI on productivity, innovation and entrepreneurship | OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-effects-of-generative-ai-on-productivity-innovation-and-entrepreneurship_b21df222-en.html)) Rowan University describes practical examples such as summarizing email and creating a first draft of presentation slides from a document, while noting that the slides may still need review and editing. ([Ways AI can improve our world](https://irt.rowan.edu/about/news/2024/10/ai-benefits.html))

- **Productivity and business performance.** *To my knowledge, unverified here:* a 2026 survey of nearly 750 corporate financial executives reportedly found positive AI-related productivity effects, with larger gains in high-skill services and finance. The reported finding that executives' perceived productivity gains exceeded gains inferred from revenue and employment data is also unverified here; the cited paper is preliminary, not peer-reviewed. These claims should be treated as evidence of possible early effects, not a settled estimate of AI's economy-wide impact. ([Artificial Intelligence, Productivity, and the Workforce: Evidence from Corporate Executives](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34984/w34984.pdf))

- **New products, revenue, and cost reductions.** *To my knowledge, unverified here:* the NBER paper reportedly finds productivity gains more closely associated with developing or improving products and serving customers than with cost-cutting alone. A 2026 NVIDIA survey reportedly found that respondents commonly cited operational efficiency and employee productivity as AI goals, and that many reported revenue or cost impacts. These are survey responses, not independently measured causal effects; NVIDIA also sells AI products, so its results are best read as a company-sponsored snapshot. ([Artificial Intelligence, Productivity, and the Workforce: Evidence from Corporate Executives](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34984/w34984.pdf); [How AI Is Driving Revenue, Cutting Costs and Boosting Productivity for Every Industry in 2026](https://blogs.nvidia.com/blog/state-of-ai-report-2026))

- **Value for a subset of companies, not yet broadly shared.** *To my knowledge, unverified here:* BCG reports that a small group of "future-built" companies is seeing much larger revenue and cost gains than other firms, while many report little material value despite investment. PwC's 2026 business predictions similarly distinguish modest efficiency or capacity gains from the harder-to-achieve outcomes of business transformation. Both accounts point to a gap between using AI and redesigning work around it; they are consulting-firm research, not a universal measure of realized returns. ([Are You Generating Value from AI? The Widening Gap | BCG](https://www.bcg.com/publications/2025/are-you-generating-value-from-ai-the-widening-gap); [2026 AI Business Predictions](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-predictions.html))

- **Changes to work, not just fewer tasks.** *To my knowledge, unverified here:* PwC's 2026 Jobs Barometer reportedly finds faster productivity growth at companies more exposed to AI, alongside faster-changing skill requirements and growth in some roles. The NBER executive survey's reported findings of little evidence of near-term aggregate employment declines, anticipated workforce reductions at larger firms, and a relative decline in routine clerical work compared with skilled technical roles are also unverified here. If accurate, these findings suggest that AI's current labor-market value and cost may include shifting what workers do and what skills jobs require — not simply replacing workers. ([AI Jobs Barometer | PwC](https://www.pwc.com/gx/en/services/ai/ai-jobs-barometer.html); [Artificial Intelligence, Productivity, and the Workforce: Evidence from Corporate Executives](https://www.nber.org/system/files/working_papers/w34984/w34984.pdf))

**What is less established**

The evidence does not show that every AI deployment pays off, or that general-purpose chat tools are reliably producing economy-wide transformation. The OECD review says results vary by task, user experience, and context, and flags gaps in evidence on long-term business effects and workers' ability to recognize AI's limitations. ([The effects of generative AI on productivity, innovation and entrepreneurship | OECD](https://www.oecd.org/en/publications/the-effects-of-generative-ai-on-productivity-innovation-and-entrepreneurship_b21df222-en.html)) PwC's business predictions make a similar distinction: small efficiency gains can be real, but transformation requires focusing on important workflows and changing how the work is done. ([2026 AI Business Predictions](https://www.pwc.com/us/en/tech-effect/ai-analytics/ai-predictions.html))

**Strategic summary:** AI is creating real but uneven value today — mainly by augmenting particular tasks and workflows, and in some cases supporting productivity, product development, and customer service. The most grounded test is not whether an organization uses AI, but whether a defined workflow improves in measurable ways after accounting for quality, human review, and the costs of implementation.

### Build a knowledge base

Upload documents — PDF, Word, Markdown, plain text, JSON, CSV — to build a private knowledge base, then ask questions about them in the same chat. Vidya searches across everything you've uploaded and answers with source attribution.

### Create a subagent

Subagents are specialized personas you create for a particular domain — a "Finance Analyst," a "Support Triage Agent," whatever fits your use case. Describe what you want and Vidya generates its name, icon, and persona for you. Each subagent keeps its own conversation memory, separate from your main chat and from every other subagent.

A subagent can also be connected to your own AWS S3 bucket with a one-click setup (a pre-filled CloudFormation stack you launch in your own AWS account) so it can read and write files there directly — Vidya never holds standing access to your bucket, only a scoped role for that one subagent.

### Connect your tools

Connect Vidya to Slack, Notion, Jira, Asana, Stripe, Canva, Zapier, or any other service that supports the Model Context Protocol — not just a fixed list, any compliant server. Once connected, just ask in plain language: *"search #project-alpha for the latest update and write a summary page in Notion."*

Anything that only reads information (a search, a fetch) runs immediately. Anything that changes something outside Vidya — sending a message, creating a page, updating a record — is held for your explicit approval first. You'll see exactly what it's about to do before it happens, and you can approve it, reject it, or opt a specific tool into running automatically once you trust it.

## Privacy & your data

- An uploaded dataset or document is held in memory (or a short-lived cache) for your session — it's never written to a database.
- Chat message text is saved so your history persists, with personal information redacted before anything is stored in long-term memory.
- Generated charts and computed results are available to download from the chat itself, but aren't kept beyond that.
- A write-capable action on a connected tool never runs without your approval unless you've explicitly opted it into running automatically.
